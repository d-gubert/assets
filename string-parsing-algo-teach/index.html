String Parsing, By Hand

No libraries. No regex. One character at a time.

1: Tab #1* (3 panes) [382x99] [layout 66e3,382x99,0,0{127x99,0,0,0,127x99,128,0,2,126x99,256,0,1}] @0 (active)

Everything in this workspace points at that one line of tmux output. It is a deceptively good teaching example: it has fields containing their own delimiter, a genuinely recursive sub-language, and a real parsing ambiguity that the tmux source resolves by backtracking. Parse it properly and you can parse most things.

Lessons

  1. The Cursor Position, peek, advance. Parse 382x99.
  2. Expect & fail well Column-accurate errors. Parse 382x99,0,0.
  3. Two phases: tokens vs. syntax Where the lexer ends and the parser begins.
  4. Recursive descent {} and [] nesting → the pane tree.
  5. Lookahead & backtracking Pane id, or width of the next cell? The real tmux ambiguity.
  6. The messy outer grammar Names with spaces and #. The full line.
  7. Grammar notation EBNF, and turning a rule into a function mechanically.
  8. Round-tripping & validation Serialise back out; verify the checksum.
  9. Design your own DSL Capstone.

Lessons are written one at a time, paced to what you have actually demonstrated. Ask for the next one when you have finished the current exercise.
