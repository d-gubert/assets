# Lisdexamfetamine: withdrawal text in regulatory labels

This file gives the label text verbatim. The text comes from the label PDFs or web pages that [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) lists. All sources were accessed on 2026-10-14.

## United States: Vyvanse prescribing information (FDA)

Version: Revised 04/2026 (NDA 021977, supplement 052). Source: Drugs@FDA PDF and DailyMed set ID `704e4378-ca83-445c-8b45-3cfa51c1ecad` ("Updated April 30, 2026").

**Section 9.3 Dependence**

> **Physical Dependence**
> VYVANSE may produce physical dependence. Physical dependence is a state that develops as a result of physiological adaptation in response to repeated drug use, manifested by withdrawal signs and symptoms after abrupt discontinuation or a significant dose reduction of a drug. Withdrawal signs and symptoms after abrupt discontinuation or dose reduction following prolonged use of CNS stimulants including VYVANSE include dysphoric mood; depression; fatigue; vivid, unpleasant dreams; insomnia or hypersomnia; increased appetite; and psychomotor retardation or agitation.

The section also says: "VYVANSE may produce tolerance."

**Section 8.1 Pregnancy, Fetal/Neonatal Adverse Reactions**

> Monitor infants born to mothers taking amphetamines for symptoms of withdrawal such as feeding difficulties, irritability, agitation, and excessive drowsiness.

**Medication Guide**

> VYVANSE may lead to physical dependence after prolonged use, even if taken as directed by your healthcare provider.

The Medication Guide and section 17 give no instruction to taper the dose.

### Earlier US wording

Label of 01/2015 (approval package for NDA 021977/S036, binge-eating disorder approval, 1/30/2015):

> Physical dependence (a state of adaptation manifested by a withdrawal syndrome produced by abrupt cessation, rapid dose reduction, or administration of an antagonist) may occur in patients treated with CNS stimulants including VYVANSE. Withdrawal symptoms after abrupt cessation following prolonged high-dosage administration of CNS stimulants include extreme fatigue and depression.

The label of 01/2022 (NDA 021977/S048) has the same "extreme fatigue and depression" text. It starts with "VYVANSE may produce physical dependence from continued therapy."

**Change:** The 2015 and 2022 text links withdrawal to "prolonged high-dosage administration". The 2026 text links withdrawal to "prolonged use" at any dose, and it lists eight symptoms. The change occurred between the 01/2022 and 04/2026 versions. The FDA class-labeling action for stimulants of 2023-05-11 standardized the abuse, misuse and addiction sections. This search did not find the FDA document that names the new 9.3 text, so the exact date of the change is not confirmed.

## European Union / United Kingdom: Elvanse SmPC

Version: Elvanse 50 mg hard capsules, date of revision of the text 14/01/2026 (emc product 14101).

**Section 4.4**

> Abuse of amfetamines can lead to tolerance, and psychological dependence with varying degrees of abnormal behaviour.

> Withdrawal symptoms such as fatigue and depression have been reported.

**Section 4.2**

> [The physician should] re-evaluate the usefulness of Elvanse at least yearly [...] consider trial periods off medication to assess the patient's functioning without pharmacotherapy.

**Section 4.6**

> Newborns exposed to amfetamine during pregnancy may experience withdrawal symptoms.

### EU safety review that added the neonatal text

PSUSA/00010289/201902, CMDh position October 2019 (EMA document created 2019-11-18). The PRAC took into account "two case reports of withdrawal syndrome in newborns of women treated with lisdexamphetamine during pregnancy, from the PSUR period". The PRAC also noted that "withdrawal symptoms have been reported with lisdexamphetamine use" and that "Withdrawal symptoms are also a potential risk in the RMP". New package leaflet text:

> Newborns exposed to amphetamine during pregnancy may experience withdrawal symptoms (trembling, irritability, tight muscle tone).

## Australia: Vyvanse Product Information (TGA)

Version: PI V12.0 (CCDS V40), date of revision 01 April 2026.

**Boxed text**

> Careful supervision is required during withdrawal from abusive use since severe depression may occur. Withdrawal following chronic therapeutic use may unmask symptoms of the underlying disorder that may require follow-up.

**Section 4.4, Drug abuse and dependence**

> Should psychological dependence occur, gradual withdrawal of the medication is recommended. Abrupt cessation following prolonged high dosage results in extreme fatigue and mental depression; changes have also been noted on the sleep EEG.

**Section 4.6**

> Infants born to mothers taking amphetamines should be monitored for symptoms of withdrawal such as feeding difficulties, irritability, agitation and excessive drowsiness.

## New Zealand: Vyvanse data sheet (Medsafe)

Version: date of revision 07 September 2026. The withdrawal text is the same as the Australian PI (boxed text, "gradual withdrawal of the medication is recommended", "extreme fatigue and mental depression; changes have also been noted on the sleep EEG", neonatal monitoring).

## Canada: Vyvanse Product Monograph (Health Canada)

Version: date of revision 20 March 2024 (initial authorization 19 February 2009).

**Section 7, Dependence/Tolerance**

> Abrupt cessation following prolonged high dosage administration results in extreme fatigue and mental depression; changes are also noted on the sleep EEG. Careful supervision is therefore recommended during drug withdrawal.

**Section 7, Suicidal Behavior and Ideation**

> [...] it is recommended for patients treated with ADHD drugs that caregivers and physicians monitor for signs of suicide-related behavior, including at dose initiation/optimization and drug discontinuation.

**Section 7.1.1, Pregnant Women**

> Infants born to mothers dependent on amphetamines have an increased risk of premature delivery and low birth weight. Also, these infants may experience symptoms of withdrawal as demonstrated by dysphoria, including agitation, and significant lassitude.

**Patient Medication Information**

> Doctor supervision is needed when you or your child stop taking VYVANSE. Suddenly ending treatment when taking higher doses of VYVANSE for a long period of time can cause: extreme fatigue, depression, changes in sleep patterns.

The patient text also says that suicidal thoughts "may occur at any time during treatment, particularly at the start or during dose changes, and also after stopping VYVANSE."
